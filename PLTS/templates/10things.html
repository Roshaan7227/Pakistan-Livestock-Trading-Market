10 Things to Check Before Buying a Cow

Livestock Buying Guide

1. Overall Health

Ensure a Healthy and Profitable Purchase:

  • Bright and clear eyes (no discharge)
  • Clean and moist nose
  • Normal breathing (no coughing or wheezing)
  • Shiny, smooth coat
  • Good appetite and alertness

2. Physical Appearance

Check the cow's body condition and physical attributes:

  • Body condition score (not too thin or fat)
  • Strong legs and clean hooves
  • Healthy udder with no swelling
  • Active and responsive behavior

3. Health History

Review the cow's medical background and records:

  • Ask for vaccination records and treatment history
  • Ensure the cow has been regularly vaccinated and dewormed
  • Request veterinary certificates if available

4. Breed and Lineage

Choose a breed suitable for your purpose:

  • Select dairy (Holstein, Jersey) or meat (Angus, Hereford) breeds
  • Ask about the cow's lineage and parent performance

5. Milk Production

Check productivity if buying a dairy cow:

  • Check daily milk yield and lactation stage
  • Average dairy cows produce 20-30 liters per day

6. Age Verification

Verify the cow's age accurately:

  • Check teeth condition (younger cows have 8 temporary incisors)
  • Request birth records when possible

7. Reproductive Status

Check pregnancy and calving history:

  • Review pregnancy history and calving records
  • A healthy cow should calve every 12-14 months
  • Ask about any breeding difficulties

8. Hooves & Legs

Ensure mobility and structural health:

  • Ensure strong legs without swelling or cracks
  • Lameness affects productivity and requires expensive treatment

9. Behavior & Temperament

Choose a manageable animal:

  • Choose calm and manageable animals
  • Aggressive cows can be dangerous and affect milk production

10. Market Price

Ensure you're getting good value:

  • Compare prices with market value before final purchase
  • Don't rush - visit multiple sellers to get the best value
